# Academic foundations for the method article, by Petteri Laine

Date: 2026-04-03

## Working note

VAA1 should adopt `constellational support` as an operational paradigmatic ontology for cue formation, detection, and multimodal claim-making.

This principle is intended to align:

- Whiteheadian process philosophy
- Bhaskarian critical realism

## Core idea

No cue should be treated as self-sufficient truth.

Cues, detections, and larger claims should be understood as:

- processual rather than static
- relational rather than atomistic
- fallible rather than self-verifying
- indicative of deeper generative structures rather than exhausted by surface appearance

## Operational meaning

VAA1 should treat a cue, detection, or claim as meaningful when it is supported by a patterned relation among multiple distinct functionaries.

Support is therefore:

- not only additive
- not limited to three constituents
- dependent on relation, co-presence, timing, and patterned interaction

This is why `constellational support` is preferable here to a narrower notion such as simple `triangulation`.

## Immediate implication for visual cues

The current `Visual cues` tool should be treated as provisional and should enter an active calibration and feedback loop.

These cues should not be treated as standalone truth claims.

They should be checked against other functionaries such as:

- transcript and linguistic analysis
- OCR and on-screen text evidence
- audio and prosodic evidence
- timeline and transition structure
- metadata and source context
- manual correction and annotation

## Program-wide implication

Constellational support should likely become a general VAA1 design principle across:

- cue formation
- confidence assignment
- fallback behavior
- UI wording
- correction workflows
- multimodal reasoning

## Working formulation

VAA1 treats cues, detections, and claims as meaningful through patterned support among multiple distinct functionaries, understood as processual and fallible manifestations of deeper generative structures.
